## The main idea
**Key line: My experience is not simply across many schools; it is across different school environments, traditions, student profiles and organisational cultures.**

That breadth has required adaptability while reinforcing a set of constants in my practice: strong relationships, high expectations, inclusion, clear subject knowledge, purposeful structure and finding an accessible entry point for the individual learner.

## Comprehensive schools
Comprehensive schools were introduced in Ireland in the 1960s as a broader model of post-primary education, bringing academic, practical and vocational traditions together within one school serving a wider community.

**St Patrick's Comprehensive School, Shannon** opened in 1966 as Ireland's first comprehensive school. This tradition is particularly relevant to me because I am also a product of comprehensive education through Crescent College Comprehensive in Limerick.

## Community schools
Community schools developed shortly after the first comprehensives. They share the broad idea of serving a whole community and offering a wide curriculum, but developed through a distinct governance and trusteeship model.

My experience in community-school settings has added to my understanding of collaborative structures, broad student provision and the importance of pastoral and community relationships.

## ETB / vocational tradition
The vocational tradition historically placed stronger emphasis on practical and technical education. In the modern system, many schools in this tradition are operated by Education and Training Boards and offer the full range of Junior and Senior Cycle programmes.

My experience in ETB and community-college settings has included mixed-ability teaching, learning support, AEN provision, LCA, JCSP, Coding, Digital Media Literacy and broad curricular pathways.

## Voluntary secondary schools
Voluntary secondary schools form another major tradition in Irish post-primary education. Many have long-established religious or voluntary patronage and historically had a strong academic identity, although modern provision is much broader.

I have worked in several voluntary secondary settings and have experienced different combinations of academic expectations, pastoral structures, subject cultures and school ethos.

## Fee-paying and boarding environments
Fee-paying and boarding are best understood as features of a school environment rather than separate statutory school types.

My experience in Villiers broadened my perspective again: a co-educational environment with a distinct Church of Ireland tradition and both day and boarding provision.

## Schools I have experienced
This section will continue to develop around the schools in my professional history, with each profile showing the school type, ethos or patronage, subjects and roles I held there, student context, and the main professional learning I carried forward.

The purpose is not to rank schools. It is to understand how different environments shaped my practice and strengthened my ability to adapt.

## A principle that carries across every setting
**Start with the learner, not the label.**

Use the available support information. Work with AEN staff, SNAs and EAL colleagues where appropriate. Adjust access through scaffolding, concrete and visual materials, technology, pace and task or assessment design. Keep the core learning and the ambition visible, while progressively building independence.
