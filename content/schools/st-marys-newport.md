## School identity | What do I know about St Mary's Newport?

St Mary's is a **Catholic, co-educational voluntary secondary school** in Newport, Co. Tipperary, under the trusteeship of **CEIST**. It is a non-DEIS school, offers Transition Year, and the current Department register records roughly **725 students**.

| Identity | Structure | Current scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Catholic** | **Voluntary secondary** | **About 720–725 students** |
| **Co-educational** | **CEIST** | **Growing school** |

**Catholic → co-ed → voluntary secondary → CEIST → growing**

## CEIST | What does being a CEIST school mean?

CEIST gives the school a shared Catholic educational framework rather than a generic label. The five values I would remember are **spiritual and human development, quality in teaching and learning, respect for every person, community, and justice and responsibility**.

| Person | Learning | Community |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spiritual and human development** | **Quality teaching and learning** | **Creating community** |
| **Respect for every person** |  | **Justice and responsibility** |

**person → learning → community → justice**

## Mission and ethos | How would I describe the school's ethos?

The school describes its purpose as providing **high-quality teaching and learning for the community it serves**, while catering for the full range of academic aptitude and abilities. I would connect the ethos to care, respect, high expectations, full development of the person and helping students reach their potential.

| Learning | Person | Community |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High-quality education** | **Full development** | **Service** |
| **Full range of ability** | **Potential** | **Catholic ethos** |

**quality → potential → care → community**

## History | What is the school's background?

The 2024 Whole-School Evaluation records that St Mary's was **founded in 1949 by the Sisters of Mercy**. It later moved to lay leadership and is now under CEIST trusteeship, so its current identity combines its Mercy heritage with the wider CEIST charter.

| Origin | Development | Today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1949** | **Lay leadership** | **CEIST** |
| **Sisters of Mercy** | **Board of Management** | **Co-educational** |

**Mercy → development → CEIST**

## Growth | Is the student body growing or falling?

It is clearly **growing**. The 2024 Whole-School Evaluation explicitly said numbers had grown rapidly and recorded 721 students; current sources still place enrolment at roughly 720-plus. The school is also progressing additional accommodation, including classrooms and specialist rooms.

| Evidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Rapid recent enrolment growth** | **Capacity pressure** |
| **About 720+ students** | **Large mixed school** |
| **Additional accommodation** | **Continued development** |

**growth → capacity → expansion**

## Current post | What do I know about the Mathematics vacancy?

The current advert is a **22-hour specific-purpose maternity-leave post** requiring Mathematics **up to and including Leaving Certificate Higher Level**, with an intended commencement date of 5 October 2026. That means the interview is likely to test both strong subject knowledge and the ability to take over established classes quickly and maintain continuity.

| Contract | Subject demand | Interview implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **22 hours** | **Mathematics** | **Take over smoothly** |
| **Maternity leave** | **LC Higher Level** | **Strong planning and subject knowledge** |

**continuity → subject knowledge → adapt quickly**

## Curriculum | What does the curriculum tell me about the school?

The school offers a broad Junior Cycle, optional **Transition Year**, the established Leaving Certificate and **LCVP**. Its own material emphasises subject choice, key skills, wellbeing and a broad range of academic, practical, technological, creative and language subjects.

| Junior Cycle | Transition Year | Senior Cycle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Broad core** | **Optional** | **Leaving Certificate** |
| **Choice subjects** | **Sampling and experience** | **LCVP** |

**breadth → choice → progression**

## Teaching and learning | What did the 2024 Whole-School Evaluation say?

The inspection found the **standard of teaching very good overall**, with positive student-teacher interactions, strong questioning and generally good engagement. The most useful interview message is that the school already has a strong base and is deliberately trying to make learning more active, formative and student-owned.

| Strength | Development |
| --- | --- |
| **Very good teaching** | **Written formative feedback** |
| **Strong questioning** | **Sharper learning intentions** |
| **Positive interactions** | **Broader active learning** |

**strong practice → formative evidence → active learning → ownership**

## Planning and SSE | What planning priorities came from inspection?

The inspection identified **subject department planning** as an area for development and asked for SSE strategies to translate more clearly into changed classroom practice. The school subsequently created a subject-planning team and a more consistent planning template.

| Inspectors wanted | School response |
| --- | --- |
| **Stronger subject planning** | **Planning team** |
| **SSE embedded in practice** | **Common structure** |
| **Evidence of impact** | **More consistent review** |

**plan → implement → evidence → review**

## Inclusion | What do I know about AEN and inclusion?

The 2024 evaluation described the school as **very inclusive and positive** and noted two autism special classes at that time, with students with autism and SEN included effectively in mainstream lessons. The useful interview connection is high expectations with appropriate support, not separate lowered expectations.

| Provision | Classroom principle | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Specialist support** | **Mainstream inclusion** | **Participation** |
| **AEN team** | **Differentiation** | **Independence** |

**support → include → challenge → independence**

## Wellbeing and behaviour | What stood out about student culture?

Inspectors described student behaviour as **exemplary** and the support for wellbeing as very strong. They highlighted positive relationships, broad extra-curricular and co-curricular opportunities, student leadership and a safe, caring environment.

## Panel questions | What useful questions could I ask the panel?

I would ask something that shows interest in the school's next stage rather than something easily found on the website. Good areas are the impact of enrolment growth, the building programme, Mathematics department priorities, and what successful continuity would look like in this maternity-leave post.

| Area | Possible question |
| --- | --- |
