### Learning-outcome map
This is a compact planning index. The official specification gives the full wording and identifies the additional Higher Level outcomes.

#### Unifying strand
- **U.1–U.3 — Building blocks:** understand and use fundamental concepts and procedures accurately, including equality as a relationship.
- **U.4 — Representation:** move flexibly between numerical, algebraic, graphical, physical and verbal representations.
- **U.5–U.6 — Connections:** connect ideas across mathematical strands and connect mathematics with real-world contexts.
- **U.7–U.10 — Problem solving:** mathematise situations, decompose and solve problems, interpret answers and evaluate alternative solutions or limitations.
- **U.11–U.12 — Generalisation and proof:** form conjectures from examples and generate or evaluate mathematical arguments and proofs.
- **U.13 — Communication:** justify reasoning and communicate conclusions using precise mathematical language and notation.

#### Number
- **N.1 — Numbers and operations:** represent and operate with numbers in ℕ, ℤ, ℚ and ℝ as appropriate; work with indices, surds, factors, multiples, prime numbers, accuracy and scientific notation.
- **N.2 — Rational-number representations:** move between fractions, decimals and percentages; use ratio and apply mathematics to financial contexts.
- **N.3 — Proportionality:** use absolute and relative comparison and solve proportional problems including currency conversion and speed–distance–time.
- **N.4 — Numerical patterns:** analyse and continue patterns using representations such as tables and graphs.
- **N.5 — Sets:** define and represent sets and use notation and operations including ∈, ∅, subset, complement, union, intersection and set difference.

#### Geometry and trigonometry
- **GT.1 — Measure and time:** calculate, interpret and apply units of measure and time.
- **GT.2 — 2D and 3D geometry:** use scale, nets, perimeter, area, surface area and volume for appropriate plane figures and solids.
- **GT.3 — Geometrical proof:** use the prescribed constructions, definitions, axioms, theorems, corollaries and converses and engage with proof.
- **GT.4 — Trigonometry:** evaluate and use sin, cos and tan and their inverses in right-angled triangles.
- **GT.5 — Coordinate geometry:** work with distance, midpoint, slope, intersections and straight-line equations such as y = mx + c, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) and ax + by + c = 0.
- **GT.6 — Transformations:** recognise and construct translations, central symmetry, axial symmetry and rotations, and identify axes of symmetry.

#### Algebra and functions
- **AF.1 — Patterns and relationships:** represent and generalise linear, quadratic and exponential-type patterns in tables, graphs, words and algebra.
- **AF.2 — Variables and expressions:** generate and interpret expressions, substitute values and use equality to form and interpret equations.
- **AF.3 — Algebraic operations:** simplify, expand, divide and factorise appropriate algebraic expressions.
- **AF.4 — Equations and inequalities:** select graphical, numerical and algebraic strategies for linear, quadratic and simultaneous equations and linear inequalities.
- **AF.5 — Quadratics from roots:** generate quadratic equations from given integer roots.
- **AF.6 — Formulae:** use inverse operations and order of operations to change the subject of a formula.
- **AF.7 — Functions:** understand function language and notation, connect graphical, verbal, diagrammatic and algebraic representations, and interpret graphs in context.

#### Statistics and probability
- **SP.1 — Chance experiments:** construct sample spaces systematically and use counting methods in authentic problems.
- **SP.2 — Random events:** interpret probability on the 0–1 scale, calculate probabilities for equally likely outcomes and use relative frequency as an estimate.
- **SP.3 — Statistical investigations:** pose statistical questions, obtain representative data, classify and display data, calculate and interpret summaries, and critique conclusions and misleading uses of statistics.

### Teaching approach
Conceptual understanding, multiple representations, reasoning, problem solving, formative assessment and accessible entry points. I use worked examples and deliberate variation where fluency is needed, but I also want students explaining why a method works and recognising when it applies.

## Leaving Certificate Mathematics
**Key line: I teach the mathematics, not merely the paper — but I also make the demands of the examination explicit and teach students how to show their reasoning clearly.**

I have experience teaching Leaving Certificate Mathematics at both Ordinary and Higher Level, including senior-cycle preparation, revision, examination technique and confidence-building with students whose prior attainment varies widely.

- [Leaving Certificate Mathematics — Curriculum Online](https://www.curriculumonline.ie/senior-cycle/senior-cycle-subjects/mathematics/)

### Ordinary Level
Emphasise secure concepts, dependable procedures, interpretation, sensible checking, mathematical language and repeated successful retrieval while maintaining meaningful problem solving.

### Higher Level
Develop deeper connection-making, flexible representation, proof and reasoning, multi-step problem solving and the ability to select methods independently under examination conditions.

## LCA Mathematical Applications
**Key line: Start with useful mathematics in authentic contexts and build accuracy, confidence, independence and mathematical judgement from there.**

I have taught LCA Mathematical Applications. The course is applied and practical, so I connect mathematics with everyday life, work, money, measurement, data and problem solving rather than presenting it as a reduced version of Leaving Certificate Mathematics.

- [LCA Mathematical Applications — Curriculum Online](https://www.curriculumonline.ie/senior-cycle/lca/mathematical-applications/)

## Transition Year Mathematics
TY gives room for mathematical investigations, modelling, data, technology, puzzles, problem solving and extension beyond an examination sequence. I use it to strengthen reasoning and confidence while showing students mathematics they can explore rather than merely reproduce.

## Digital and interactive mathematics
GeoGebra, Desmos, interactive web apps, visualisation and carefully chosen technology where it improves understanding. Technology should expose structure or support exploration; it should not replace the mathematical thinking.
