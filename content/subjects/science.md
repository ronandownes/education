## Junior Cycle Science — Learning outcomes
**Key line: Nature of Science is the unifying strand; inquiry, evidence, communication and science-in-society should run through the four contextual strands.**

This is a compact planning index rather than a substitute for the specification. The official outcomes describe end-of-three-year learning and their numbering supports planning; it is not a prescribed teaching order.

### Strand 1 — Nature of Science
- **1.1 Understanding science:** appreciate how scientists work and how scientific ideas change as evidence develops.
- **1.2 Investigable questions:** recognise questions suitable for scientific investigation, frame testable hypotheses and compare possible investigative approaches.
- **1.3 Planning investigations:** design and conduct investigations while considering reliability, accuracy, precision, fairness, safety, ethics and equipment choice.
- **1.4 Evidence and conclusions:** generate or select qualitative and quantitative data, identify patterns and anomalies, and justify conclusions.
- **1.5 Reflection and transfer:** review the thinking and skills used in investigations and apply them in unfamiliar problem-solving contexts.
- **1.6 Research:** investigate scientific issues and evaluate sources and secondary data for quality, detail and possible bias.
- **1.7 Communication:** organise and communicate scientific findings for purpose and audience using appropriate terminology and representations.
- **1.8 Media claims:** evaluate arguments about science and technology presented in the media.
- **1.9 Scientists and society:** research scientists' contributions to discovery and invention and their effects on society.
- **1.10 Science in society:** consider the personal, social and global importance of science and the ways society influences scientific research.

### Strand 2 — Earth and Space
- **2.1 Celestial relationships:** describe relationships among moons, asteroids, comets, planets, stars, solar systems, galaxies and space.
- **2.2 Origin of the universe:** explore scientific models used to represent the origin of the universe.
- **2.3 Earth in the solar system:** interpret data comparing Earth with other planets and moons, including mass, gravity, size and composition.
- **2.4 Earth–Sun–Moon system:** use a model to explain seasons, lunar phases and solar and lunar eclipses.
- **2.5 Cycling matter:** describe carbon and water cycles and connect them with biological and atmospheric processes.
- **2.6 Energy needs:** research energy sources and form an evidence-based view of current and future energy needs.
- **2.7 Climate:** connect Earth processes and human factors with climate, climate change impacts and possible responses.
- **2.8 Space exploration:** examine benefits, hazards and future implications of space exploration.

### Strand 3 — Chemical World
- **3.1 Conservation of mass:** investigate mass during physical and chemical changes.
- **3.2 Particle model:** use models of the atomic nature of matter to explain states, changes, mixtures, separation and conservation of mass.
- **3.3 Atomic structure:** model atoms using nucleus, protons, neutrons and electrons and compare their charge and mass.
- **3.4 Classification of matter:** classify substances as elements, compounds, mixtures, metals, non-metals, solids, liquids, gases and solutions.
- **3.5 Periodic Table:** use the Periodic Table to predict atom ratios in compounds formed from two elements.
- **3.6 Material properties:** investigate properties such as solubility, conductivity, melting point and boiling point.
- **3.7 Reaction rates:** investigate variables that affect rates of chemical reactions, including common gas-producing and biochemical reactions.
- **3.8 Acids and bases:** investigate acid–base reactions and use indicators and the pH scale.
- **3.9 Energy changes:** describe reactions using exothermic, endothermic and activation-energy ideas and interpret simple energy profiles.
- **3.10 Materials and sustainability:** evaluate extraction, use, disposal and recycling of materials in relation to sustainability.

### Strand 4 — Physical World
- **4.1 Measurement:** select and use appropriate measuring instruments.
- **4.2 Physical quantities:** measure or calculate length, mass, time, temperature, area, volume, density, speed, acceleration, force, potential difference, current, resistance and electrical power.
- **4.3 Relationships:** investigate patterns and relationships between physical observables.
- **4.4 Physics and technology:** research a technological application of physics and discuss scientific, societal and environmental impacts.
- **4.5 Circuits:** design and build simple electronic circuits.
- **4.6 Energy conservation:** explain energy conservation and analyse energy transformations and dissipation.
- **4.7 Energy devices:** design, build and test a device that transforms energy, then evaluate energy changes and possible efficiency improvements.
- **4.8 Electricity and sustainability:** research ethical and sustainability issues arising from electricity generation and consumption.

### Strand 5 — Biological World
- **5.1 Cells:** investigate plant and animal cell structures and relate structures to functions.
- **5.2 Reproduction and inheritance:** describe sexual and asexual reproduction and explore inheritance and variation.
- **5.3 Evolution:** outline evolution by natural selection and connect it with biological diversity.
- **5.4 Human systems:** describe the structure, function and interactions of digestive, circulatory and respiratory systems.
- **5.5 Ecology:** investigate a habitat and examine adaptation, competition and interdependence.
- **5.6 Human health:** investigate how inherited factors, environment, nutrition, lifestyle and microorganisms affect health.
- **5.7 Respiration and photosynthesis:** investigate these processes and factors that affect them.
- **5.8 Ecosystem flows:** explain the movement of matter and energy through ecosystems.
- **5.9 Human reproduction:** describe human sexual reproduction and consider related medical, ethical and societal issues.
- **5.10 Biodiversity and food:** evaluate biodiversity conservation and global food production in relation to ecosystem benefits and sustainability.

## Science interview anchors
- **Inquiry with structure:** practical work should move from a clear question through evidence to explanation; activity alone is not the learning goal.
- **Nature of Science throughout:** students should repeatedly question evidence, evaluate sources, communicate findings and refine explanations.
- **Safety is planned, not improvised:** risk, equipment, movement, grouping and laboratory routines are considered before practical work begins.
- **Assessment supports learning:** questioning, observation, practical evidence, student explanations, CBAs and summative assessment give different evidence of progress.
- **Scientific language matters:** explicitly teach the vocabulary, diagrams, units, conventions and sentence structures students need to explain ideas precisely.

## Inclusion in Science
Clear procedures, visual supports, structured practical roles and careful group organisation help make laboratory learning accessible and safe. I can vary the route into the task — demonstration, model, diagram, scaffolded method, vocabulary support or paired practical role — while keeping the scientific learning intention intact.
