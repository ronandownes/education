## General approach | What is your general approach to AEN and inclusion?


| Understand Experience | Support | Review |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Learner need** | **Match intervention to barrier** | **Check impact** |
| **Intended learning** | **Maintain challenge** | **Build independence** |
|  |  |  |


**My interventions are needs-led rather than diagnosis-led.** I approach this through **Understand, Support and Review**. I start with the **learner, not the label**, and identify the barrier and the intended learning. I then match the intervention to the barrier — for example, **visual supports, tactile or concrete materials, clearer chunking, additional processing time, or a calm and predictable classroom environment**. I keep the intended learning and level of challenge in view, and review whether the support is improving access, participation and ultimately independence.



## Autism support | How do you support autistic students with different needs?

**My interventions are needs-led rather than diagnosis-led.** I do not assume that every autistic student needs the same provision. I begin with the learner's strengths, interests and perspective; their Student Support File and agreed supports; observations of what helps and what creates barriers; and consultation with the student, the SEN/AEN team and parents or guardians where appropriate. I maintain high expectations and review the effect of any intervention.

### Three questions: Regulation · Accessibility · Challenge

| Regulation | Accessibility | Challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Can the student engage safely and comfortably?** | **Can the student access, participate in and demonstrate the learning?** | **Is the learning appropriately demanding?** |
| Consider anxiety, sensory load, transitions, overload and agreed regulation supports. | Consider communication, task initiation, executive functioning, clear instructions, representation and response options. | Match mathematical demand to the student's knowledge and potential: reasoning, connections, generalisation and proof. |

**Ask all three questions for each learner.** They are three *independent dimensions*, not three categories of autistic students. A highly achieving student may still have substantial sensory, communication or regulation needs. A student experiencing distress may be capable of sophisticated mathematical thinking when barriers are removed. Being quiet or compliant does not necessarily mean a student is coping.

### Match the intervention to the barrier

| Barrier I identify | Possible responsive action in my Mathematics classroom |
| --- | --- |
| **Emotional regulation** — anxiety, frustration, overload, shutdown or meltdown | Recognise early signs, stay calm, reduce demands and language during distress, offer the *agreed* break or regulation strategy, follow the student's support plan and school safety procedures, and re-engage when ready. |
| **Sensory differences** — noise, light, movement, proximity | Reduce avoidable sensory and visual clutter, review seating and activity demands, and use agreed sensory adjustments. |
| **Transitions and uncertainty** | Use a predictable lesson sequence, make changes explicit, give advance notice where possible and explain what happens next. |
| **Communication differences** — literal interpretation or lengthy instructions | Use concise, direct language; support it with mathematical notation, visuals and worked examples; allow processing time; check understanding discreetly. |
| **Executive-function barriers** — starting, sequencing, organising or changing tasks | Make the first step visible, chunk the problem, use a checklist or prompt, check in discreetly and gradually fade support when appropriate. |
| **Social interaction and group work** | Structure roles and collaboration; establish respectful classroom norms; offer an appropriate way to contribute without unnecessary public pressure. |
| **High mathematical attainment** | Give depth rather than repetitive extra work: justification, pattern-finding, generalisation, connections, unfamiliar problems and proof. Continue other supports if needed. |

### Two contrasting presentations — and why they can overlap

**Significant regulation needs.** I aim to make learning safe, predictable and accessible. I identify possible triggers with the learner and SEN team; establish routines; minimise avoidable sensory demands; and use calm, agreed strategies. During distress I prioritise safety and co-regulation over repeated public instructions or confrontation. I return to learning sensitively afterwards.

**High attainment or high mathematical potential.** I don't equate autism with low academic expectations. I use appropriately demanding tasks that develop reasoning and conceptual depth, while recognising that the same student may need support with organisation, transitions, communication or regulation.

**Mathematics example:** After securely expanding binomials, rather than assigning more routine exercises, I could ask a student to derive and explain

\[
(x+a)(x+b)=x^2+(a+b)x+ab
\]

and to investigate how the middle coefficient changes when \(a\) and \(b\) are positive, negative or equal. **Extend through complexity and insight, not merely quantity.**

### Spoken interview answer

I would start by saying that **my interventions are needs-led rather than diagnosis-led**. Autism is a spectrum, and I don't assume that two autistic students will experience the same barriers. I would learn about the student's individual strengths and needs through the Student Support File, student voice, classroom observation and collaboration with the SEN team.

I organise my response around three questions: **Regulation, Accessibility and Challenge**. Can the student engage comfortably? Can they access the task and show their learning? And is the work sufficiently challenging? If regulation is difficult, I focus on predictability, sensory considerations and agreed calming strategies. If communication or executive functioning creates a barrier, I use clear instructions, visual supports, structured tasks, processing time and discreet check-ins.

Some autistic students are exceptionally able in Mathematics. I would extend them through deeper problems, mathematical reasoning and proof, while remembering that high attainment does not cancel other support needs. I review whether my approach improves engagement, learning, wellbeing and independence. **I aim to remove barriers, not lower expectations.**

### Quick recall

| Identify | Respond | Evaluate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Individual strengths and barriers** | **Regulation · Accessibility · Challenge** | **Engagement · Learning · Independence** |
| Student voice, Student Support File, observation, SEN-team input | Agreed supports, accessible teaching, appropriate mathematical depth | Check impact and adjust; don't assume a diagnosis predicts need |

**One-line retrieval:** *Needs-led, not diagnosis-led. Regulation, Accessibility, Challenge. Remove barriers without lowering expectations.*

Use the guidance alongside the individual learner's agreed support plan and the school's procedures.

## STAR AEN — Scaffold to independence


| STAR | Recall |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Situation** | A student could begin Maths only with frequent adult prompting. |
| **Task** | Improve access while reducing dependence. |
| **Action** | I made the first step explicit, chunked the task, used a visual prompt and gradually reduced prompts as the student became more secure. |
| **Result** | The student began more tasks independently and needed less adult support. |


## STAR AEN — Barrier versus ability


| STAR | Recall |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Situation** | A student appeared weak on written tasks but showed stronger understanding orally. |
| **Task** | Work out whether the main difficulty was conceptual or an access barrier. |
| **Action** | I simplified unnecessary language, used visual support and allowed an oral explanation while keeping the mathematical demand unchanged. |
| **Result** | The student demonstrated stronger conceptual understanding and I could target the actual literacy barrier more precisely Through Liasing |


## Identifying need | How do you identify a student's educational needs?


| Evidence | Interpret | Plan |
| --------------- | ------------- | -------------------- |
| **Records** | **Strengths** | **Priority need** |
| **Observation** | **Barriers** | **Support response** |


I approach this through **Evidence, Interpret and Plan**. I triangulate information rather than relying on one label or score. I use the learner profile, Student Support File, previous work, assessment evidence, observation, student voice and relevant professional information to identify strengths, barriers and the support most likely to help.



## Continuum of Support | How do you use the Continuum of Support?


| Support for All | Support for Some | Support for Few |
| ----------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Inclusive classroom** | **Targeted support** | **Individualised plan** |
| **Monitor progress** | **Review response** | **Specialist input** |


I approach this through **Support for All, Support for Some and Support for Few**. The Continuum of Support helps match the **intensity of support to the level of identified need**. I begin with effective inclusive classroom practice, add targeted support where evidence shows it is needed and move to more individualised support only when the learner requires it.



## Student Support File | How do you use a Student Support File effectively?


| Profile | Plan | Review |
| ------------------ | -------------- | ------------- |
| **Strengths** | **Targets** | **Evidence** |
| **Priority needs** | **Strategies** | **Next step** |


I approach this through **Profile, Plan and Review**. A Student Support File should be a **working planning document**, not a filing exercise. It brings together the learner's strengths, priority needs, targets, agreed supports and review evidence so that teachers, support staff, parents and the student can work coherently.



## Classroom inclusion | What does inclusive practice look like in a mainstream lesson?


| Access | Participation | Independence |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Clear language** | **Active involvement** | **Fade support** |
| **Useful representation** | **Check understanding** | **Transfer responsibility** |


I approach this through **Access, Participation and Independence**. Inclusive practice means students can **participate meaningfully in the same learning community**. I make the learning clear, reduce unnecessary barriers, use accessible representations and scaffolds, check participation and fade support as students become more secure.



## Supporting attention and executive function | How do you support students with ADHD or executive-function difficulties?


| Start | Structure | Release |
| -------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Clear first step** | **Chunk task** | **Reduce prompts** |
| **Visible routine** | **Processing time** | **Build self-management** |


I approach this through **Start, Structure and Release**. I reduce unnecessary executive load without reducing the learning demand. I make the first step explicit, chunk the task, use visible routines and prompts, give processing time and gradually shift organisation back to the student.



## Literacy and dyslexia | How do you support dyslexia or significant literacy difficulty?


| Access text | Build language | Show learning |
| ---------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Clear layout** | **Explicit vocabulary** | **Appropriate response** |
| **Oral support** | **Visual support** | **Processing time** |


I approach this through **Access text, Build language and Show learning**. I remove literacy barriers that are not part of the intended learning while still developing subject language. I use clear layout, explicit vocabulary, oral explanation, visual support and additional processing time, and I allow appropriate ways for students to show what they know.



## Numeracy difficulty | How do you support dyscalculia or significant numeracy difficulty?


| Diagnose | Represent | Secure |
| -------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------- |
| **Prerequisite gap** | **Concrete model** | **Structured practice** |
| **Misconception** | **Visual model** | **Check transfer** |


I approach this through **Diagnose, Represent and Secure**. I identify the specific mathematical barrier rather than assuming a general inability. I return to prerequisite concepts, use concrete or visual representations, reduce unnecessary cognitive load, provide structured practice and check whether understanding transfers beyond the scaffold.



## Communication and language | How do you support speech, language or communication needs?


| Make clear | Give time | Check |
| ------------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Concise language** | **Processing time** | **Private check** |
| **Pre-teach vocabulary** | **Visual support** | **Confirm meaning** |


I approach this through **Make clear, Give time and Check**. I make communication predictable and accessible. I use concise language, pre-teach important vocabulary, allow processing time, support meaning with visuals and check understanding without putting the student under unnecessary public pressure.



## Sensory and regulation | How do you support sensory or regulation needs?


| Anticipate | Regulate | Re-engage |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Notice triggers** | **Use agreed support** | **Return to task** |
| **Predictable routine** | **Reduce overload** | **Build self-regulation** |


I approach this through **Anticipate, Regulate and Re-engage**. I try to understand triggers and early signs rather than waiting for crisis. I use agreed regulation supports, reduce unnecessary sensory load, keep routines predictable and help the student return to learning, with the long-term aim of stronger self-regulation.



## Team teaching and support | How do you use team teaching or small-group support effectively?


| Plan | Teach | Review |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Shared goal** | **Defined roles** | **Evidence of impact** |
| **Likely barrier** | **Targeted support** | **Adjust provision** |


I approach this through **Plan, Teach and Review**. Support should be planned around a **shared learning purpose**, not simply an extra adult in the room. We agree roles, likely barriers and evidence of learning; small-group or withdrawal support is used when it has a clear purpose and its impact is reviewed.



## Working with an SNA | How do you work effectively with an SNA?


| Clarify | Coordinate | Release |
| ------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Learning goal** | **Share observations** | **Reduce dependence** |
| **Agreed support** | **Consistent approach** | **Promote independence** |


I approach this through **Clarify, Coordinate and Release**. I work collaboratively while keeping roles clear. I share relevant learning goals and agreed supports, listen to the SNA's observations and ensure assistance promotes participation, dignity and independence rather than unnecessary adult dependence.



## L1LP and L2LP | How do you plan for students following Level 1 or Level 2 Learning Programmes?


| Outcomes | Experience | Evidence |
| --------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------- |
| **Priority learning** | **Meaningful context** | **Authentic task** |
| **Relevant target** | **Active participation** | **Record progress** |


I approach this through **Outcomes, Experience and Evidence**. I plan from the relevant **Priority Learning Units and learning outcomes**, connect them to meaningful contexts and gather evidence through authentic tasks. The emphasis is functional learning, participation, communication and progression rather than simply simplifying mainstream worksheets.



## High expectations | How do you avoid additional needs becoming low expectations?


| Access | Challenge | Release |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Remove barrier** | **Require thinking** | **Fade scaffold** |
| **Provide support** | **Expect progress** | **Increase independence** |


I approach this through **Access, Challenge and Release**. I separate **access support from intellectual demand**. I provide the scaffold needed to enter the learning, but I still ask students to think, explain, practise and improve; then I fade support when evidence shows they are ready.



## Reviewing support | How do you know whether AEN support is working?


| Evidence | Judge | Decide |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | ------------ |
| **Progress** | **Impact** | **Continue** |
| **Student voice** | **Independence** | **Adapt** |


I approach this through **Evidence, Judge and Decide**. I look for evidence of improved **participation, attainment, regulation and independence** against the agreed target.
