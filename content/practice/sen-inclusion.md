## What is your approach to SEN / AEN?
**Key line: Support participation. Build independence. Keep ambition high.**

I start with the learner rather than the label. I use available student-support information, collaborate with the AEN team and other staff, and adapt access through structure, chunking, modelling, concrete and visual materials, technology, reduced cognitive load and additional processing time where needed.

Support should increase participation and independence rather than create unnecessary dependence.

## How do you differentiate in a mixed-needs class?
**Key line: Same core learning; different routes, supports and levels of challenge.**

I vary entry point, scaffolding, representation, pace, task complexity and how students can show understanding. I also plan extension for students who are ready to move further, so inclusion does not mean lowering expectations.

## What experience do you have of learning support?
I have worked in mainstream classes, small-group withdrawal, learning support and collaborative settings. I have used technology and concrete materials extensively in small-group support as well as in mainstream classes with very varied learning profiles.
