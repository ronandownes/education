## General planning | What is your general approach to planning?

I start with the **students**, the **intended learning** and the **evidence** that will show me whether learning has happened. I then sequence the learning, plan support and challenge, and keep the plan flexible enough to respond to what students show me.

| Start | Build | Respond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Students** | **Sequence learning** | **Check evidence** |
| **Learning intention** | **Support / challenge** | **Adapt next step** |

**students → learning → sequence → evidence → adapt**

## Planning scales | How do you plan across a year, unit, week and lesson?

The principles stay the same; the **time horizon changes**. The year gives the route, the unit sequences a body of learning, the week keeps progression coherent and the lesson decides the immediate next step.

| Year | Unit | Week / lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Map the route** | **Sequence concepts** | **Next learning step** |
| **Pace the programme** | **Assessment points** | **Teach, check, adapt** |

**year → unit → week → lesson → adapt**

## Year plan | If you were given a Maths class in September, how would you plan the year?

I begin with the **curriculum**, the class starting point and the time available. I map the main learning across the year, build in assessment, retrieval and consolidation, and keep enough flexibility to respond to actual progress.

| Start | Map | Revisit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Curriculum / class** | **Sequence / pace** | **Retrieval** |
| **Time available** | **Assessment points** | **Consolidate / adapt** |

**curriculum → starting point → sequence → pace → assess → revisit**

## Unit or difficult topic | How would you plan a unit or a difficult mathematical topic?

I identify the **prerequisites** and likely misconceptions first. Then I break the learning into manageable steps, choose examples and representations carefully, build practice in stages and check whether students can apply the idea independently.

| Foundations | Build | Secure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prerequisites** | **Examples / representations** | **Practice** |
| **Misconceptions** | **Model / explain** | **Check transfer** |

**prerequisites → misconceptions → represent → model → practise → transfer**

## Lesson / cover lesson | How would you plan an individual lesson, including a cover lesson?

I establish what students already know, what they should learn next and what success will look like. I plan a short sequence of explanation or modelling, student practice and checks for understanding. For a cover lesson, I keep the structure especially clear, achievable and self-contained.

| Start | Teach | Finish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Starting point** | **Explain / model** | **Check** |
| **Learning intention** | **Practise** | **Consolidate** |

**starting point → learning intention → teach → practise → check → consolidate**

## Taking over a class | How would you take over a class from another teacher?

I use **handover information** to understand what has been taught, how secure it is and any important learner needs. I then confirm that picture through early retrieval, observation and student work rather than assuming the handover tells me everything.

| Handover | Confirm | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scheme / point reached** | **Retrieval** | **Next learning** |
| **Needs / supports** | **Observation / work** | **Adjust pace** |

**handover → confirm → diagnose → continue → adapt**

## Curriculum alignment | How do the specification and learning outcomes shape your planning?

I plan from the **curriculum rather than the textbook**. I unpack the relevant outcomes, pay attention to the action verbs, and use them to decide the depth of learning, success criteria and evidence I need.

| Curriculum | Meaning | Planning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Learning outcomes** | **Know / understand / do** | **Sequence** |
| **Action verbs** | **Depth** | **Evidence / tasks** |

**specification → outcomes → action verbs → depth → evidence**

## Scheme and department | How do schemes of work and department planning influence your planning?

The department plan gives the broad agreed approach; the **scheme of work** maps learning for a particular year or class. I contribute through shared sequencing, resources, assessment evidence and common expectations while still using professional judgement for the students in front of me.

| Agree | Share | Apply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core outcomes / sequence** | **Resources / evidence** | **Professional judgement** |
| **Common expectations** | **Misconceptions / approaches** | **Respond to class** |

**agree → share → sequence → apply → adapt**

## Assessment and adaptation | How does assessment evidence change your planning?

I build **formative assessment into the plan**, not onto the end of it. If learning is secure I move on or increase challenge; if it is not, I re-teach, change representation, revisit a prerequisite or give more guided practice.

| Evidence | Diagnose | Respond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Questioning** | **Secure** | **Move on** |
| **Observation** | **Uncertain** | **Increase challenge** |
| **Student work** | **Gap** | **Re-teach** |
| **Mini-whiteboards** | **Misconception** | **Change representation** |
| **Short check** |  | **Revisit prerequisite** |
| **Exit task** |  | **Guided practice** |

**check → diagnose → respond → check again**

## Inclusion and challenge | How do you plan for mixed ability and additional educational needs?

I keep a **common worthwhile learning goal** where possible and vary the route into it. I anticipate barriers, plan suitable scaffolds and representations, and increase challenge through reasoning and unfamiliar application rather than simply giving more work.

| Access | Support | Challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Know the learner** | **Scaffold / model** | **Reason / justify** |
| **Anticipate barriers** | **Represent differently** | **Generalise / apply** |

**learner → barrier → support → challenge → independence**

## TY Maths | How would you plan a Transition Year Mathematics programme?

I would use the freedom of TY to broaden students' experience of Mathematics rather than simply move ahead into Leaving Certificate content. I would build a flexible programme around **problem-solving, financial maths, data, modelling and projects**, using technology where it genuinely adds value.

| Purpose | Content | Experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Broaden Mathematics** | **Finance / data / modelling** | **Projects / investigation** |
| **Build confidence** | **Problem-solving** | **Useful technology** |

**broaden → apply → investigate → create → review**

## Other programmes | How would your planning change across Junior Cycle, Senior Cycle, LCA or Level 1 / Level 2?

The **planning principles stay consistent**, but the purpose, pacing, assessment and level of abstraction change. I plan from the requirements of the programme and then adjust the sequence, context, support and evidence of learning to suit the learners.

| Programme | Main emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| **Junior Cycle** | **Outcomes / reasoning / connections** |
| **Senior Cycle** | **Level / depth / exam demands** |
| **LCA / Level 1–2** | **Relevant / accessible / purposeful** |

**same principles → different purpose → pacing → assessment → experience**

## Coverage and consolidation | How do you balance coverage, depth, retrieval and revision?

I want **coverage with learning**, not coverage for its own sake. I prioritise foundations, revisit important learning through retrieval and cumulative review, and use formative evidence to decide when to move on and when a short revisit is necessary.

| Cover | Revisit | Decide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core learning** | **Retrieval** | **Move on** |
| **Progression** | **Cumulative review** | **Revisit foundation** |

**prioritise → retrieve → consolidate → check → progress**

## Continuity | How do you ensure continuity and progression from one teacher or year to the next?

I use schemes of work, assessment records and clear handover to identify both **what was taught and how securely it was learned**. I connect new learning to those foundations, confirm them early and then build the natural next step.

| Know | Confirm | Progress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What was taught** | **Retrieval / evidence** | **Next step** |
| **How secure it is** | **Gaps / strengths** | **Revisit or extend** |

**record → handover → confirm → connect → progress**

## Review and improve | How do you evaluate and improve your planning?

I compare what I intended with **what students actually learned**. I review pacing, assessment evidence, misconceptions, resources and support or challenge, then keep, move, remove or strengthen what needs changing for the next cycle.

| Review | Decide | Improve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Learning evidence** | **Keep / move** | **Refine sequence** |
| **Pacing / misconceptions** | **Remove / strengthen** | **Improve next plan** |

**evidence → reflect → decide → refine**
