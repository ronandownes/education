## Lesson design | How do you plan a typical lesson with learning intentions, success criteria and engagement?

| Start | Learning sequence | Check and finish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Curriculum outcome** | **Explain and model** | **Question / exit ticket** |
| **Prior knowledge / barriers** | **Guided → independent work** | **Evidence / next step** |
| **Learning intention / success criteria** | **Active reasoning and discussion** | **Consolidation** |

I begin with the curriculum outcome and the **students in front of me**. What do they already know? What prerequisites are secure? What barriers or misconceptions might arise? I define a clear **learning intention** and **observable success criteria** so pupils know what they are learning and can judge how well they are doing. I plan a coherent sequence: activate prior knowledge, explain or model, provide guided practice, let students think and work independently or collaboratively, then check individual understanding. Engagement is not just keeping pupils busy: they need to **explain, reason, make decisions and receive feedback**. I finish with consolidation or an **exit ticket** that influences the next lesson.

**Concrete example:** In First Year Sets, I can sequence familiar examples and listing elements before set-builder language, universal sets, complements and two-set Venn diagrams. Each step checks understanding before introducing more notation.

## Curriculum and progression | How do you align lessons and schemes of work with curriculum outcomes?

| Curriculum | Scheme of work | Continuity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Specification / learning outcomes** | **Prerequisite sequence** | **Retrieval and revision** |
| **Action verbs / mathematical processes** | **Time, resources and assessment** | **Evidence and handover** |

I **plan from the specification rather than simply following a textbook chapter**. I unpack learning outcomes and their action verbs: are students expected to calculate, interpret, represent, reason, justify or solve unfamiliar problems? The department plan and scheme of work provide coherence across the year. I organise a logical progression of prerequisites, concepts and applications, with time for practice, assessment, retrieval and consolidation. I monitor **what has actually been learned**, not just what has been covered, and use records and handover information to preserve continuity between lessons, teachers and years. The sequence is planned, but the pace remains responsive.

**Concrete example:** In algebra, I can move from multiplying two linear expressions and identifying patterns in the resulting quadratic to factorisation and the relationship between factors and roots.

## Inclusive planning | How do you plan for mixed attainment and students with SEN or AEN?

| Know the learner | Remove barriers | Review access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strengths and learning profile** | **Accessible materials / representations** | **Participation and understanding** |
| **SEN team / support records / observations** | **Chunking, modelling, processing time** | **Fade scaffold; build independence** |
| **Targeted challenge for all** | **Choice of support** | **Evaluate intervention** |

I start by understanding **the actual learning profile of the learner in front of me**, not by assuming that a diagnosis or one test score tells the whole story. I draw on classroom observation, students' work and voice, relevant support records and collaboration with the SEN team. I identify the specific **difficulties and barriers** that could prevent a fair opportunity to access and demonstrate learning. I aim to remove **unnecessary barriers**, not simply lower the mathematics: I use clear language, accessible layouts, visual or tactile representations, chunked tasks, additional processing time and suitable grouping where these help. I plan meaningful **challenge** as well as support. My homework can offer **optional, graduated scaffolding**: a student may attempt independently and check the answer, or reveal worked steps. I then check what the student can do without that scaffold and adapt it. **Fair does not always mean identical**; the goal is access, progress and growing independence.

**Concrete example:** A student may manage the arithmetic in a trigonometry problem but struggle to identify the sides and select a ratio. I can scaffold **identify the angle → label the sides → select the ratio → calculate**, and gradually remove the prompts while keeping the same mathematical objective.

## Assessment and flexibility | How do you build assessment into planning and adapt when a lesson goes differently?

| Elicit | Interpret | Respond and check again |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prior-knowledge task / hinge question** | **Misconception or access barrier?** | **Re-model / scaffold / extend** |
| **Mini-whiteboards / written steps** | **Reasoning and vocabulary** | **Exit ticket / independent check** |

Assessment is **designed into the lesson**, not added as a test at the end. I plan questions, opportunities to observe students explaining their thinking, individual written attempts, mini-whiteboards and **exit tickets**. Looking at intermediate steps tells me *where* a misunderstanding occurs, so I can provide **specific, actionable feedback**. I assess mathematical reasoning and students' **receptive and productive mathematical vocabulary**, as well as computational accuracy. If a task or explanation is not working, I do not rigidly follow the plan: I pause, check which prerequisite or representation is causing difficulty, explain it differently, adapt the scaffolding or adjust the pace. If learning is secure, I deepen the challenge. I **reassess** before deciding what comes next. My plan is **a route, not a script**.

## Literacy numeracy and technology | How do you plan mathematical language, reasoning and effective digital learning?

| Language | Mathematical thinking | Digital choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Receptive vocabulary** | **Interpret and represent** | **Visualise a concept** |
| **Productive vocabulary** | **Calculate, reason and justify** | **Interactive practice and feedback** |

Mathematical literacy is part of my planning from the outset. **Receptive vocabulary** means understanding terms and instructions in a problem; **productive vocabulary** means explaining reasoning orally or in writing using increasingly precise mathematical language. I allow an initial explanation in everyday language, then help students refine it. **Computation is only one part of Mathematics**: learners must interpret questions, select methods, justify solutions and communicate mathematical processes. I choose digital technology **because it improves learning**, not for novelty. GeoGebra can help pupils explore a representation, and my interactive Mathematics resources can offer staged explanations and self-checks. I would use digital responses as formative evidence where the system supports that. Any collection or comparison of identifiable student results requires school approval and appropriate data protection; it is not a substitute for my professional judgement.

## Reflective planning | How do you reflect on a lesson and improve your next plan?

| Compare | Learn | Act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intention versus evidence** | **Where did learning stall?** | **Adapt next lesson** |
| **Engagement versus understanding** | **Whose needs were missed?** | **Refine examples / support** |

After the lesson I ask whether **students achieved the learning intention**, not merely whether I completed the activities. I review responses, worked steps, exit tickets and student questions. I consider whether the sequencing, examples, pace, vocabulary, scaffolding and challenge were appropriate. I ask which pupils remained unsure, whether the barrier was conceptual or one of access, and what I should do differently. I use those findings to **refine the next lesson, revisit a foundation, change the representation or adjust the homework**. I am also open to colleague feedback. Effective planning is an **ongoing cycle**, not a document completed once.

## General planning | What is your general approach to planning?

| Start | Build | Respond |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Students** | **Sequence learning** | **Check evidence** |
| **Learning intention** | **Support / challenge** | **Adapt next step** |

I approach planning through **Start, Build and Respond**. I begin with the **intended curriculum learning** and **the actual students in front of me**: prior knowledge, vocabulary, strengths and barriers to access. I then plan a carefully sequenced route with clear learning intentions, success criteria, appropriate supports and mathematical challenge. I decide in advance **how I will obtain individual evidence** of understanding, then adapt the lesson from that evidence rather than treating the plan as a rigid script.

| **Learning intention** | **Practise** | **Consolidate** |

I plan a short sequence of explanation or modelling, student practice and checks for understanding. For a cover lesson, I keep the structure especially clear, achievable and self-contained.



## Taking over a class | How would you take over a class from another teacher?

| Handover | Confirm | Plan |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Scheme / point reached** | **Retrieval** | **Next learning** |
| **Needs / supports** | **Observation / work** | **Adjust pace** |

I approach this through **Handover, Confirm and Plan**. I use **handover information** to understand what has been taught, how secure it is and any important learner needs. I then confirm that picture through early retrieval, observation and student work rather than assuming the handover tells me everything.

| Evidence | Diagnose | Respond |
| -------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Questioning** | **Secure** | **Move on** |
| **Observation** | **Uncertain** | **Increase challenge** |
| **Student work** | **Gap** | **Re-teach** |

| **Short check** |  | **Revisit prerequisite** |

I approach this through **Evidence, Diagnose and Respond**. I build **formative assessment into the plan**, not onto the end of it. If learning is secure I move on or increase challenge; if it is not, I re-teach, change representation, revisit a prerequisite or give more guided practice.

## Inclusion and challenge | How do you plan for mixed ability and additional educational needs?

| Know the learner | Remove the barrier | Retain challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Actual learning profile** | **Accessible tasks / flexible scaffold** | **Shared worthwhile objective** |
| **Observation / SEN team / records** | **Clear steps / representations / time** | **Review and fade support** |

I begin by understanding the **individual learner in front of me**: strengths, current attainment and the specific barriers they face. I use observation, work samples, relevant school information, the student's perspective and collaboration with the SEN team rather than assuming a label or CAT4 score dictates an approach. I plan to **remove unnecessary barriers so each student has a fair opportunity to learn**, while maintaining appropriate mathematical expectations. That may mean shorter instructions, a visual model, staged questions, more processing time or optional worked solutions. I also plan extension through justification, generalisation and unfamiliar applications. I review whether the support works and gradually **transfer responsibility back to the learner**.

## Other programmes | How would your planning change across Junior Cycle, Senior Cycle, LCA or Level 1 / Level 2?

| Programme | Main emphasis |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Junior Cycle** | **Outcomes / reasoning / connections** |
| **Senior Cycle** | **Level / depth / exam demands** |
| **LCA / Level 1–2** | **Relevant / accessible / purposeful** |

I approach this through **Programme and Main emphasis**. The **planning principles stay consistent**, but the purpose, pacing, assessment and level of abstraction change. I plan from the requirements of the programme and then adjust the sequence, context, support and evidence of learning to suit the learners.



## Coverage and consolidation | How do you balance coverage, depth, retrieval and revision?

| Cover | Revisit | Decide |
| ----------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Core learning** | **Retrieval** | **Move on** |
| **Progression** | **Cumulative review** | **Revisit foundation** |

I approach this through **Cover, Revisit and Decide**. I want **coverage with learning**, not coverage for its own sake. I prioritise foundations, revisit important learning through retrieval and cumulative review, and use formative evidence to decide when to move on and when a short revisit is necessary.



## Continuity | How do you ensure continuity and progression from one teacher or year to the next?

| Know | Confirm | Progress |
| -------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------- |
| **What was taught** | **Retrieval / evidence** | **Next step** |
| **How secure it is** | **Gaps / strengths** | **Revisit or extend** |

I approach this through **Know, Confirm and Progress**. I use schemes of work, assessment records and clear handover to identify both **what was taught and how securely it was learned**. I connect new learning to those foundations, confirm them early and then build the natural next step.



## Review and improve | How do you evaluate and improve your planning?

| Review | Decide | Improve |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Learning evidence** | **Keep / move** | **Refine sequence** |
| **Pacing / misconceptions** | **Remove / strengthen** | **Improve next plan** |

I approach this through **Review, Decide and Improve**. I compare what I intended with **what students actually learned**. I review pacing, assessment evidence, misconceptions, resources and support or challenge, then keep, move, remove or strengthen what needs changing for the next cycle.
