## General approach | How do you differentiate in a mixed-ability class?


| Common | Access | Extend | Aim |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Learning intention** | **Clear entry point** | **Reasoning** | **Participation** |
| **Core task** | **Scaffold** | **Connections** | **Independence** |
| **High expectations** | **Representation** | **Unfamiliar application** |  |


I approach this through **Keep common, Access, Extend and Aim**. I keep the **important learning common** while varying the route into it. I begin with a common learning intention and design a task with a **clear entry point and room for depth**. I use scaffolding and representation for access, while extending through **reasoning, connections and unfamiliar application**. The aim is high expectations, participation and growing independence.

## Entry point | How do you choose an appropriate entry point?


| Check | Start | Build |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Prerequisite** | **Accessible example** | **Increase complexity** |
| **Misconception** | **Useful representation** | **Move to abstraction** |


I approach this through **Check, Start and Build**. I identify the **prerequisite knowledge** and test it quickly rather than assuming it is secure. I then choose the simplest starting representation or example that exposes the new idea without lowering the eventual level of demand.

## Scaffolding | How do you scaffold without creating dependence?


| Support | Check | Fade |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| **Prompt** | **Student attempt** | **Remove cue** |
| **Worked example** | **Evidence of security** | **Transfer responsibility** |


I approach this through **Support, Check and Fade**. A scaffold should make the next step possible, not complete the thinking for the student. I use prompts, worked examples, visual cues or partial structures, then reduce them as soon as evidence shows the learner can continue independently.

## Multiple representations | How do you use representations to improve access?


| Represent | Connect | Transfer |
| ------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Visual** | **Compare forms** | **Use independently** |
| **Symbolic** | **Explain connection** | **Choose representation** |


I approach this through **Represent, Connect and Transfer**. I choose representations that reveal the structure of the concept: numerical, algebraic, graphical, geometric or visual. I then help students move between them, because the goal is not merely to make something easier but to strengthen conceptual understanding.

## UDL | How does Universal Design for Learning fit your teaching?


| Design | Access | Maintain |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Anticipate barrier** | **Multiple route in** | **Common goal** |
| **Clear information** | **Flexible response** | **Success criteria** |


I approach this through **Design, Access and Maintain**. UDL encourages me to remove predictable barriers before they become individual problems. I make key information clear, offer useful ways to engage and respond, and still keep the learning goal and success criteria explicit.

## Cognitive load | How do you reduce cognitive load without reducing challenge?


| Reduce | Focus | Build |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Unnecessary detail** | **Key information** | **Small steps** |
| **Competing demands** | **Worked example** | **Independent attempt** |


I approach this through **Reduce, Focus and Build**. I remove **unnecessary difficulty**, not the thinking itself. I sequence carefully, keep explanations concise, highlight what matters, use worked examples where appropriate and avoid overloading students with competing information.

## Questioning | How do you differentiate through questioning?


| Support | Probe | Extend |
| ------------------ | ----------- | ------------------- |
| **Prompt** | **Explain** | **Generalise** |
| **Representation** | **Justify** | **Compare methods** |


I approach this through **Support, Probe and Extend**. I vary the **support and depth of the question**, not the respect shown to the learner. Some students may need a prompt or representation; others can be asked to justify, generalise or compare methods, while everyone remains part of the same mathematical conversation.

## Pace and grouping | How do pace and grouping support differentiation?


| Group | Pace | Reconnect |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Targeted support** | **Pause when needed** | **Common learning** |
| **Purposeful pairing** | **Extend when secure** | **Review together** |


I approach this through **Group, Pace and Reconnect**. I use flexible grouping and pacing when they improve learning, not as fixed labels. I may pause a small group for targeted support, pair students for explanation or let secure learners move to deeper work, while checking that everyone remains connected to the same core learning.

## High attainers | How do you challenge high-attaining students?


| Deepen | Connect | Apply |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Justify** | **Generalise** | **Unfamiliar problem** |
| **Compare methods** | **Make connection** | **Create example** |


I approach this through **Deepen, Connect and Apply**. I avoid giving simply more of the same. I increase challenge through **depth, proof, generalisation, connection, efficiency and unfamiliar application**, and I ask students to communicate and defend their reasoning.

## Productive struggle | How do you know when to help and when to let a student struggle?


| Observe | Prompt | Release |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Is thinking continuing?** | **Smallest useful cue** | **Step back** |
| **Is barrier blocking access?** | **Restore entry point** | **Check independence** |


I approach this through **Observe, Prompt and Release**. I distinguish productive struggle from unproductive confusion. If the student has a plausible route and is still thinking, I give time; if the barrier prevents engagement, I use the smallest prompt that restarts the thinking and then step back again.

## Accessibility and standards | How do you make learning accessible without lowering standards?


| Preserve | Remove | Check |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Learning goal** | **Irrelevant barrier** | **Same standard** |
| **Success criteria** | **Access obstacle** | **Independent evidence** |


I approach this through **Preserve, Remove and Check**. I keep the **intended learning and success criteria** intact and remove barriers that are irrelevant to them. That may mean clearer language, more time, a different representation or response format, but not doing the intellectual work for the student.

## Assessment access | How do you differentiate assessment while preserving validity?


| Target | Adjust | Protect |
| --------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------ |
| **What is being measured?** | **Access feature** | **Validity** |
| **Success criteria** | **Response mode** | **Standard** |


I approach this through **Target, Adjust and Protect**. I ask what the assessment is meant to measure. If reading speed, layout or response mode is not the target, I can adjust those features while keeping the knowledge, reasoning or skill being assessed unchanged.

## Review differentiation | How do you know whether differentiation is working?


| Evidence | Judge | Adapt |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Participation** | **Access improved?** | **Change support** |
| **Independence** | **Challenge sufficient?** | **Change challenge** |


I approach this through **Evidence, Judge and Adapt**. I look for changes in **access, participation, understanding and independence**. If a scaffold is permanent, a high attainer is only doing more work or a group remains passive, I adapt the approach rather than assuming differentiation has happened.

## STAR examples | What examples could you use to show differentiation without lowering standards?

These are **rehearsal composites**: use the structure with a real lesson you have taught.

## STAR 1 — mixed-attainment Maths


| STAR | Recall |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Situation** | A mixed-attainment class needed the same core concept but very different levels of support. |
| **Task** | Keep one worthwhile learning goal while giving appropriate access and challenge. |
| **Action** | I used a common core task, visual and worked-example scaffolds for access, and deeper reasoning and unfamiliar application for students ready to extend. |
| **Result** | More students participated in the same mathematical conversation while challenge remained high. |


## STAR 2 — fading a scaffold


| STAR | Recall |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Situation** | A student was completing work accurately but only when a step-by-step scaffold was present. |
| **Task** | Test whether the support could be reduced. |
| **Action** | I removed one prompt at a time, checked the student's reasoning and restored only the minimum cue when needed. |
| **Result** | The student completed similar work with less support and greater confidence. |
