Exactly. **“Safeguard when required” is much broader than serious abuse or something criminal.**

In a school context, safeguarding means **recognising when a student’s welfare or safety may be at risk and following the school’s procedures rather than trying to deal with it alone**.

That could include noticing or becoming aware of things such as **a significant change in behaviour, persistent distress, bullying, neglect, inappropriate relationships, online safety concerns, self-harm concerns, unexplained injuries, worrying disclosures, or circumstances at home affecting the student’s safety or welfare**.

So your line has a sensible progression:

**Build trust → notice changes → support regulation and re-engagement → coordinate pastoral support → safeguard when required.**

The last part basically means: **if what I notice moves beyond ordinary pastoral support into a welfare or child-protection concern, I know when to escalate it through the proper safeguarding procedures.**

And importantly, **you are not investigating or deciding whether somebody has committed an offence**. You notice, listen appropriately, record/report as required, and pass the concern to the appropriate person.
