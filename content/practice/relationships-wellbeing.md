## Relationships and learning | What role do relationships play in learning?

| Trust | Expect | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Respect** | **High standards** | **Change in presentation** |
| **Belonging** | **Fair boundaries** | **Readiness to learn** |

I approach this through **Trust, Expect and Notice**. Positive relationships create **trust, belonging and readiness to learn**. I try to know students well enough to notice change, communicate high expectations and correct behaviour without damaging dignity; the relationship supports learning rather than replacing standards.



## STAR RW — Change in engagement

| STAR | Recall |
| --- | --- |
| **Situation** | A normally engaged student became withdrawn and stopped completing work. |
| **Task** | Support the student without diagnosing or overstepping my role. |
| **Action** | I checked in privately, listened, made a small classroom adjustment and passed the concern through the appropriate pastoral structure. |
| **Result** | The student had a clearer route to support and gradually re-engaged with class. |



## STAR RW — Repairing a relationship

| STAR | Recall |
| --- | --- |
| **Situation** | A difficult classroom incident had damaged the working relationship with a student. |
| **Task** | Re-establish boundaries and make successful re-entry possible. |
| **Action** | I spoke privately when things were calm, separated the behaviour from the person, clarified impact and agreed a practical fresh start. |
| **Result** | The relationship improved and later correction could happen with less confrontation. |



## New class | How do you establish positive relationships with a new class?

| Know | Be consistent | Build trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Learn names** | **Fair expectations** | **Notice effort** |
| **Listen** | **Follow through** | **Show interest** |

I approach this through **Know, Be consistent and Build trust**. I build relationships through ordinary consistency: learn names, greet students, be fair, listen, explain expectations and notice effort. I avoid trying to manufacture popularity; trust develops when students experience me as predictable, respectful and interested in their learning.



## Pastoral role | What is the subject teacher's pastoral role?

| Notice | Support | Refer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Change in behaviour** | **Listen** | **Pastoral structure** |
| **Change in engagement** | **Classroom adjustment** | **Specialist support** |

I approach this through **Notice, Support and Refer**. My pastoral role is to **notice, listen, support and refer appropriately**. I can create a safe classroom, check in, contribute observations and help a student reconnect with learning, while recognising when the concern needs the tutor, year head, AEN team or another professional.



## Classroom wellbeing | What does wellbeing look like in your classroom?

| Safety | Participation | Growth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Predictable routines** | **Student voice** | **Appropriate challenge** |
| **Respect** | **Help-seeking** | **Recover from mistakes** |

I approach this through **Safety, Participation and Growth**. Wellbeing is not a separate activity from learning. It is visible in **predictability, respectful relationships, participation, appropriate challenge and psychological safety**, so students can make mistakes, ask for help and still feel that the classroom is a place where they belong.



## Concern | What would you do if you were concerned about a student's wellbeing?

| Observe | Check in | Escalate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Facts** | **Private conversation** | **Pastoral route** |
| **Pattern** | **Listen** | **Safeguarding route** |

I approach this through **Observe, Check in and Escalate**. I begin with what I have actually observed and avoid diagnosing. I check in privately, listen, record significant facts and communicate through the appropriate pastoral structure; if there is a safeguarding concern, I follow child-protection procedure immediately.



## Anxiety or distress | How do you support a student who is anxious or distressed?

| Settle | Support | Re-engage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calm tone** | **Clear next step** | **Return to task** |
| **Processing time** | **Reduced pressure** | **Review support** |

I approach this through **Settle, Support and Re-engage**. I reduce immediate pressure without removing all challenge. I use calm language, predictable steps, processing time and an achievable re-entry point, then coordinate further support if the pattern is persistent.



## Regulation | How do you support emotional regulation?

| Anticipate | Regulate | Return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Notice trigger** | **Use agreed strategy** | **Re-enter learning** |
| **Predictable routine** | **Give processing time** | **Build self-regulation** |

I approach this through **Anticipate, Regulate and Return**. I try to notice triggers and early signs before behaviour escalates. I use predictable routines, reduced language, space or an agreed regulation strategy where appropriate, then help the student return to learning and gradually build self-regulation.



## Attendance | How does attendance fit your pastoral role?

| Notice | Reconnect | Communicate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pattern** | **Welcome back** | **Tutor or year head** |
| **Change** | **Catch-up route** | **Follow-up** |

I approach this through **Notice, Reconnect and Communicate**. Attendance gives useful early evidence about engagement and wellbeing. I notice patterns, welcome students back without public judgement, help them reconnect with missed learning and communicate persistent concerns through the agreed school structure.



## Peer conflict | How do you respond when peer relationships are affecting wellbeing?

| Stabilise | Understand | Resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stop escalation** | **Listen** | **Restore where possible** |
| **Protect dignity** | **Clarify impact** | **Use procedure if needed** |

I approach this through **Stabilise, Understand and Resolve**. I stop escalation, listen to the relevant accounts and avoid taking sides too quickly. I keep the focus on behaviour and impact, use restorative conversation where appropriate and involve the school's anti-bullying or pastoral procedures when the issue goes beyond ordinary conflict.



## Bullying | What would you do if a student reported bullying?

| Receive | Record | Act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Listen** | **Relevant facts** | **Follow procedure** |
| **Protect safety** | **No promise of secrecy** | **Follow up** |

I approach this through **Receive, Record and Act**. I take the report seriously, listen calmly, record the relevant facts and follow the school's **Bí Cineálta / anti-bullying procedure**. I do not run a private investigation or promise secrecy, and I make sure the student knows the concern has been acted on.



## Restorative practice | How do you use a restorative conversation?

| Understand | Acknowledge | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What happened?** | **Who was affected?** | **What changes now?** |
| **Listen** | **Accept responsibility** | **Fresh start** |

I approach this through **Understand, Acknowledge and Repair**. A restorative conversation asks what happened, who was affected and what needs to happen next. I keep boundaries clear, support accountability without humiliation and agree a realistic repair when participants are calm enough to engage.



## Professional boundaries | How do you remain supportive without becoming the student's counsellor?

| Support | Boundaries | Refer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Listen** | **Do not diagnose** | **Use support team** |
| **Check in** | **Protect confidentiality** | **Seek specialist help** |

I approach this through **Support, Boundaries and Refer**. I can notice, listen, check in and support classroom participation while keeping my role clear. I do not diagnose or take on specialist work; I share relevant concerns through the correct structure and maintain confidentiality within its proper limits.



## Parents and coordinated support | How do you work with parents and colleagues around wellbeing?

| Share | Listen | Coordinate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Factual observation** | **Family context** | **Agreed support** |
| **Progress** | **Student voice** | **Review together** |

I approach this through **Share, Listen and Coordinate**. I communicate observations rather than labels, listen to relevant context and keep the focus on the student's participation and readiness to learn. With colleagues I agree a consistent response so support is coordinated rather than dependent on one teacher.



## Safeguarding | What do you do if a wellbeing concern becomes a safeguarding concern?

| Protect | Record | Refer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Immediate safety** | **Facts** | **DLP or DDLP** |
| **Do not investigate** | **Accurate note** | **Follow procedure** |

I approach this through **Protect, Record and Refer**. Once there is a safeguarding concern, I prioritise safety, record the relevant facts and report through the child-protection procedure to the DLP or DDLP. I do not investigate independently and I do not promise confidentiality.
