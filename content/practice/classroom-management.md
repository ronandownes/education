## What is your general approach to classroom management?
**Key line: Hope. Expectation. Trust.**

My approach is preventative first. I establish clear routines and expectations, teach them explicitly, and build a relationship in which students know that I am fair, calm and interested in their progress.

I do not treat every difficulty as the same problem. Low-level disruption may need proximity, a reminder, a change of task or a quiet conversation. Persistent or more serious behaviour needs recording, restorative follow-up and collaboration with the relevant year head, pastoral or AEN structures. Safeguarding or serious emotional distress is a different level again and I follow school procedures immediately.

## How do you deal with low-level disruption?
**Key line: Correct early, calmly and proportionately while keeping the lesson moving.**

I use presence, proximity, names, non-verbal cues, a clear reminder of the expectation and, where useful, a short private conversation rather than turning a small issue into a public confrontation. I also ask what may be driving the behaviour: is the work inaccessible, is attention drifting, is there a peer dynamic, or is there an unmet need?

## What if behaviour becomes persistent or challenging?
**Key line: Prevent → intervene → restore → record → collaborate → escalate proportionately.**

I use the school's code and support structures. I record patterns rather than relying on memory, communicate with the relevant staff, and involve parents or support personnel at the appropriate stage. The aim is to restore learning and participation, not simply to punish.

## What about an overwhelmed student or safeguarding-type situation?
**Key line: Safety and dignity come before finishing the lesson exactly as planned.**

If a student becomes overwhelmed, I keep the situation calm, maintain appropriate supervision, use agreed supports and bring in the SNA, year head, deputy principal or other designated staff as required by the student's plan and school procedure. I do not improvise arrangements that compromise supervision or safeguarding.
