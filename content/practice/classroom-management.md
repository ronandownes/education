## What is your general approach to classroom management? q

**Key line: Hope. Expectation. Trust.**

My approach is preventative first. I establish clear routines and expectations, teach them explicitly, and build a relationship in which students know that I am fair, calm and interested in their progress.

I do not treat every difficulty as the same problem. Low-level disruption may need proximity, a reminder, a change of task or a quiet conversation. Persistent or more serious behaviour needs recording, restorative follow-up and collaboration with the relevant year head, pastoral or AEN structures. A behaviour of concern is different again: where there is a risk to the student's safety or wellbeing, or to others, my priorities become safety, regulation and de-escalation, following the school's agreed procedures and support plan.

## How do you deal with low-level disruption?

**Key line: Correct early, calmly and proportionately while keeping the lesson moving.**

I use presence, proximity, names, non-verbal cues, a clear reminder of the expectation and, where useful, a short private conversation rather than turning a small issue into a public confrontation.

I also ask what may be driving the behaviour: is the work inaccessible, is attention drifting, is there a peer dynamic, or is there an unmet need?

## What if behaviour becomes persistent or more serious?

**Key line: Prevent → intervene → restore → record → collaborate → escalate proportionately.**

I use the school's Code of Behaviour and support structures. I record patterns rather than relying on memory, communicate with the relevant staff, and involve parents or support personnel at the appropriate stage.

The aim is to restore learning and participation, not simply to punish.

## What if there is a behaviour of concern?

**Key line: Safety, regulation and de-escalation come first.**

A behaviour of concern is behaviour that indicates a risk to the safety or wellbeing of the student or of others.

If a student becomes seriously dysregulated or overwhelmed, I remain calm, reduce demands where appropriate, maintain appropriate supervision, use agreed supports and involve the relevant SNA, year head, deputy principal or other designated staff in accordance with the student's support plan and school procedures.

I do not improvise arrangements that compromise supervision or safety.
