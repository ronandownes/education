> **Synthetic-data notice:** This class is entirely imaginary. The numbers and needs below are invented for planning and interview practice and do not describe real pupils.

## Class context

8 students seen four times weekly in a small group, coordinated with mainstream Mathematics. Two sessions occur during Irish for pupils with documented exemptions.

## Strengths and interests

Practical reasoning, willingness to use manipulatives and strong peer support when tasks are short and purposeful.

## Fictional learning and access profile

3 students with dyslexia; 2 with developmental language needs; 2 with ADHD; 1 student awaiting further assessment. All show gaps in number facts, place value or mathematical vocabulary.

## Starting evidence

Screening and classroom work indicate that language load and working-memory demand often block performance before the mathematics itself is reached.

## Planned teaching response

Teach through concrete–visual–symbolic sequences; explicitly rehearse vocabulary; use short cumulative practice, read-aloud supports and worked-example fading. Align each week with mainstream learning and avoid becoming a disconnected alternative curriculum.

## Assessment and review loop

One-minute retrieval samples, observation notes, annotated work and a fortnightly transfer check using an unfamiliar but accessible problem.
