## Mathematics — teaching abstract ideas

**Question:** How do you teach abstract Mathematics, such as algebra, when concrete materials or visualisation are not sufficient?

| Start with meaning | Move to symbolic reasoning | Check understanding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patterns, structure and prior knowledge | Variables, generalisation, equivalence | Explain why, test examples and counterexamples |
| Multiple representations where they genuinely help | Precise definitions and worked examples | Misconceptions, independent problems, transfer |

I would not insist that every abstract idea must have a physical model. **Concrete and visual representations are useful bridges, not compulsory endpoints.** In algebra I would begin with meaningful numerical patterns or equivalence, connect them to symbolic notation and then explicitly teach how expressions are transformed and why the rules work. I would check understanding by asking students to explain a step, find a counterexample or solve a structurally different problem. My aim is that they understand the generality of algebra rather than memorising a sequence of procedures.
