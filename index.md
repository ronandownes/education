## Hope · Expectation · Trust

This is the organising idea behind the site. **Hope** that progress is possible; **expectation** that students engage, behave and move forward; **trust** that the classroom is fair, safe and purposeful.

## How this site is organised

The main navigation keeps the eight interview domains visible and adds two practical shelves: **Profiles** and **Plans**. **Profiles** contains both class profiles and school profiles. **Plans** contains actual schemes of work and the curriculum specifications they use. Teaching experience sits with Professional Responsibility rather than in a separate Timeline tab. **Policies** remains available from the reference shelf below.

Use the top navigation as the first place to look: interview questions stay under their domains, while learner/school context and working plans have obvious homes of their own.

### Overview

You're currently focused on preparing for an upcoming interview for a Mathematics and Learning Support/SEN teaching role in Ireland. At the same time, you're building a personal interview-preparation website and related tools that combine interview questions, curriculum guidance, synthetic class profiles, planning resources, and retrieval-based study supports. You prefer practical, structured material that is easy to revise aloud and print for reference.

### Teaching and Interview Preparation

You prefer interview preparation to be organised around clear teaching domains such as Teaching & Learning, Classroom Management, SEN/AEN & Inclusion, Assessment & Feedback, Reporting, Planning & Curriculum, School Ethos, and School Types. You like interview answers to use H2 question headings followed by concise, readable lines rather than dense paragraphs. You also prefer retrieval cues, memorable word chains, and short prompts that help you answer naturally without memorising scripts. You have asked for realistic interview questions covering topics such as lesson planning at short notice, first-year planning, formative assessment, copy review, reporting, pre-teaching vocabulary, and effective teaching.

### Interview Website Project

You're developing a GitHub-hosted website as a personal interview study tool. You prefer a clean interface with competency tabs across the top, separate sections for Classes and Plans, working navigation, printable layouts, and consistent formatting. You asked to remove unnecessary interface elements such as the 'On this page' panel and audio or line-by-line reveal controls, while keeping editing simple. You also want integrated curriculum documents, inspection summaries, school information, retrieval chains, glossary support where appropriate, and printable resources that remain uncluttered.

### Synthetic Classroom Resources

You want realistic but entirely fictional class profiles to support interview preparation. These include mainstream mathematics classes across different year groups, Learning Support groups, TY, and LCA classes. You prefer editable seating plans, fictional names, yearbook-style student photos, learner profiles, support indicators, assessment information where appropriate, handover notes, and a wide range of learning needs represented, including autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD, EAL, executive functioning needs, behavioural and emotional supports. You prefer these resources to feel authentic while remaining clearly fictional.

### Curriculum and Planning Preferences

You like curriculum planning to begin with learner profiles, prior information, and early assessment before unpacking learning outcomes into teachable steps. You prefer schemes of work that connect specifications, sequencing, retrieval practice, differentiation, formative assessment, consolidation, revision, and appropriate challenge. You have repeatedly emphasised practical classroom application over abstract descriptions and prefer examples that show how learning outcomes become individual lessons.

### Working Style

You prefer direct, practical collaboration with regular progress updates. You like responses that preserve important terminology exactly as requested, avoid unnecessary wording, and use consistent formatting throughout a project. Because you have autism and are permitted to bring notes into your interview, you prefer layouts that are easy to scan, practise aloud, and use as memory prompts, with clear line breaks and concise language rather than long prose.
