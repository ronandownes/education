  # Team Teaching
  ## Key Line

  **Two teachers should give us more teaching options, not one teacher teaching while the other permanently shadows particular students.**

  You actually already have a very good Team-teaching question in AEN:
  > How do you use team-teaching effectively for AEN?

  And the existing answer already has the right core: **shared planning → common goal → identify barriers → agree roles → flexible grouping → check understanding → compare evidence → evaluate impact.**
  ## Before the Lesson

  Agree the **learning intention**, likely barriers, which students may need support or challenge, and what each teacher is going to do.
  ## During the Lesson

  The two teachers can **change the structure according to the learning**: both circulate; one models while one checks; split the class temporarily; run parallel groups; take a targeted group; use stations; or one observe specific learning evidence.

  The important point is that **the roles can switch**. Neither teacher is “the real teacher” and neither becomes attached to the AEN students.
  ## Use the Second Teacher Strategically

  This is where these class profiles become very useful. Looking at the learner map we could say:

  **“Today I want you to take these four for ten minutes because the evidence shows they need another representation of fractions, while I move the rest into independent practice. Then we bring everybody back together.”**

  That is much more convincing in interview than simply saying “we support individual students.”
  ## Review

  At the end: **What did each of us notice? Who understood? Who still has the misconception? Did the grouping help? What do we change next time?**

  So the memorable retrieval chain is:

  **PLAN → ROLES → GROUP → TEACH → CHECK → REVIEW**

  And I think that belongs very naturally with these interactive class profiles — eventually we could even make **“Team Teaching” a mode on the learner map**, where you can drag students temporarily into Teacher A / Teacher B groups and talk through why.
